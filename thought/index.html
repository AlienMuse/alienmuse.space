…raised around them the universes in which they become natural, until, as the sea rises, they open of themselves. This Programme asks whether that rising can be continued — and learned.

Series 00 / Dedication

01The rising sea#

02Seven principles#

Read side by side, the seven works keep seven principles. Each is stated in at least four of them, in their own words.

Series 00 / §3.3

  1. 01

    Change the world before the problem.

    When a problem resists, the works do not strike harder at it; they ask what ambient world would make it natural, build that world under explicit axioms, and record what the change gains and loses.

  2. 02

    A conclusion is a section that glues.

    A conclusion is licensed only where local evidence agrees on overlaps and glues to a whole.

  3. 03

    An obstruction is an output.

    Where an argument fails, the failure is located, typed and kept.

  4. 04

    No result stronger than its proof.

    Every claim carries its status where it is made.

  5. 05

    Generation is not verification.

    The producer of a candidate is never its only judge.

  6. 06

    Abstraction is earned by what it preserves.

    A new structure earns its place by what it preserves, the obstruction it exposes and what it makes checkable.

  7. 07

    The method recurses, and it can fail.

    Each work states in advance what would refute it.

Series 00 / §3.3

03Twelve theses#

The Rising-Sea Theses

Each thesis is stated in one sentence, argued, tied to the passages of the seven works on which it rests, related to existing work, and paired with an observation that would refute it.

The Rising-Sea Theses / Abstract

  1. Thesis 01

    Worlds, not isolated theorems

    For a reasoning system, the unit of mathematical knowledge is a typed, verifiable world, not an isolated theorem.

    What would refute it

    A system that stores statements without world records and makes no more transport errors — after base change, change of coefficients or change of variance — than one that stores worlds, on a corpus of the kind specified for Problem 15.

    Open problems415

  2. Thesis 02

    Status is part of the type

    The epistemic status of a statement belongs to its type.

    What would refute it

    A scalar ordering of statements that preserves, under the composition rules of (Series 01, Vol. III, §56.15), every distinction drawn by the seven codes; or a controlled study in which a calibrated confidence score supports the same downstream decisions with no more errors.

    Open problems7831

  3. Thesis 03

    A structured latent space

    Mathematical reasoning needs a structured, non-vectorial latent space.

    What would refute it

    A vector representation that performs as well as a typed carrier on tasks requiring transport and failure reporting — the counterexample and proof strata of the benchmark of Problem 13 — or a proof that the typed structure is recoverable from the embedding at no loss.

    Open problems618

  4. Thesis 04

    Attention as descent

    Attention can be rebuilt as descent.

    What would refute it

    The null outcomes of the registered comparisons of Problems 19, 20 and 22; or a proof that transport along restriction maps adds no expressive power over attention in a stated class.

    Open problems1920222425272835

  5. Thesis 05

    Report failure; abstain without evidence

    A reasoning system should report where local information fails to glue, and abstain when independent overlap data are absent.

    What would refute it

    A system that never abstains and reports scores only, with fewer errors at equal coverage than one that reports obstructions and abstains; or a proof that the obstruction cannot be computed at useful cost (Problem 21).

    Open problems916172123303247

  6. Thesis 06

    A chain of non-implications

    Operational completion does not imply mathematical validity, which does not imply epistemic promotion, which does not imply publication.

    What would refute it

    A class of problems in which one of the passages is shown to need no separate input — for example a fidelity checker that makes validity of the formal statement imply validity of the intended mathematics (Problem 5).

    Open problems512262934

  7. Thesis 07

    Machines propose; humans authorize

    Machines may propose; humans authorize publication.

    What would refute it

    A publication process without human authorisation whose rate of retraction and correction is no higher than that of a human-authorised process on comparable work; or a capability model in which the separation cannot be enforced.

    Open problems1011

  8. Thesis 08

    The rising sea

    Hard problems are better approached by raising the surrounding theory than by direct attack.

    What would refute it

    Comparative evidence that, for a reasoning system at equal cost, direct search settles more problems than theory-building does; or the research subjects of the Programme yielding no result that the extended setting made tractable.

    Open problems13637384151525556

  9. Thesis 09

    State what is not established

    A result is not complete until it states what it does not establish.

    What would refute it

    Evidence that statements of what is not established add nothing that the hypotheses of the positive statement do not already convey — for example, that they can be generated mechanically from the hypotheses — or that readers misuse results no less often without them.

    Open problems33394043444548495354

  10. Thesis 10

    Discovery is recursive

    Mathematical discovery is recursive: propose, test against what is known, abstract what survives, ask again.

    What would refute it

    Machine runs of the recursion that, on problems where the answer is known, rediscover no known result and produce no conjecture that survives its falsifiers (Problem 3); or a body of discoveries in which no abstraction step can be identified.

    Open problems23

  11. Thesis 11

    Creating language

    A mathematical reasoning system must be able to create notation and language, not only use them.

    What would refute it

    A system restricted to a fixed language that matches a language-creating system on discovery tasks of the kind of Problem 14; or a language-creating system whose new primitives never survive review.

    Open problems1457

  12. Thesis 12

    Judge a method by its open ledger

    A method should be judged by an open ledger of what it establishes, what it reduces to stated hypotheses, and what it leaves open.

    What would refute it

    Ledgers that cannot be made checkable at the scale of a book — (Series 06) itself records that the soundness of its ledger is not established — or that readers find less informative than the positive statements.

    Open problems13424650

04A Life in Three Universes#

Alexander Grothendieck: A Life in Three Universes

The idea

One of the deepest changes associated with Grothendieck can be stated in ordinary language. Instead of asking only what a mathematical object is by itself, ask how it varies, what maps into it, what maps out of it, how it appears after a change of base, and which constructions remain compatible across those changes. The object does not disappear. It becomes intelligible through a system of relations.

Preface

The method

Heroic biography looks backward for signs: a childhood question anticipates a theorem, exile becomes the source of abstraction, the solitary child already contains the mathematician who will remake geometry. This book takes a different route. It follows documents before symbols, chronology before destiny, and relations before essence.

Preface

The boundary

One movement holds this Preface together: a change of scale can make relations visible, but every enlargement meets a boundary. That movement governs how the book explains mathematics, collective work, refusal, silence, and the archive. It also governs how the narrative stops when its evidence stops.

Preface

Three universes

  1. 01

    The lived universe

    Statelessness, war, migration, institutions, conflict, activism, withdrawal.

  2. 02

    The mathematical universe

    From isolated problem solving to languages that reorganize whole fields.

  3. 03

    The philosophical universe

    Simplicity, creation, community, responsibility, rupture, solitude, dreams.
